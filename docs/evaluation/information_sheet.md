You are invited to take part in a short evaluation session. Please read this sheet before you
decide. You can ask questions at any time.

## What is the study about?

Machine-learning detectors of Android malware can learn properties such as an app's build year
instead of what the app does. ShortcutCheck tests a trained detector for this, without needing
labelled data, and can block a release in continuous integration. We want to know whether
practitioners find it useful and understandable.

## What would I do?

A session of about 40 minutes, online or in person. You would use the tool on two small demo
models built from synthetic data, look at its dashboard, read a CI workflow, and fill in a short
questionnaire. You would be asked to think aloud. **We are evaluating the tool, not you.** You can
skip any task or question.

## Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time during the session without giving a
reason. You can withdraw your responses until [date: two weeks after your session], after which
they will have been combined with other responses for analysis.

## What data will be collected, and how will it be kept?

Your answers to the questionnaire, background information (your role, years of experience and
familiarity with CI and malware detection) and the researcher's notes. Your name appears only on
the consent form, which is stored separately from your answers. Your answers are labelled with a
code such as P03. With your permission only, the session is audio or screen recorded; recordings
are deleted within two weeks, once the notes have been checked. Results are reported in aggregate
in a dissertation and possibly an academic article; no quotation will identify you.

## Are there any risks or benefits?

The risks are minimal: the session uses synthetic data only, on the researcher's laptop. There is
no payment. You may find the tool useful in your own work.
